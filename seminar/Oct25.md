# Dynamics on Networks and Network Dynamics
Nynke Niezink, 24. October 15:00 CET (that is 14:00 UK time).

**Abstract:** Social networks play a significant role in individual decision-making. Understanding the origins and effects of people's network positions requires us to focus on the social mechanisms that drive network and individual change (e.g., in opinions, performance, or behavior). The stochastic actor-oriented model is a statistical model to study these mechanisms in network and individual dynamics based on longitudinal data. In this talk, I examine social dynamics from two perspectives. First, I consider dynamics on networks. I discuss a stochastic actor-oriented model for the co-evolution of social networks and continuous individual outcomes, representing individual attribute changes by a stochastic differential equation and network evolution by a Markov chain model. I illustrate this model, along with its close connection to non-network autoregression, through a study of how peers influence students' stress development in higher education. Second, I reflect on the study of network dynamics. A common assumption in dynamic network analyses and social network analysis in general is that people share a correct view of the network they are part of. However, research has shown that people’s perceptions of their networks can differ significantly. I discuss the importance of zooming in on these perception differences and propose a stochastic actor-oriented model for the three-way analysis of the dynamics of network perceptions.
